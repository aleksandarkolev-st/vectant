# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Developers using a Vectant support session to diagnose a local project. They are working inside a trust-sensitive desktop workflow and need to understand, at a glance, which device, workspace, session, files, logs, and localhost services are connected or shared.

## Product Purpose

Vectant Local Support is a read-only, session-scoped, workspace-scoped bridge between one local development environment and one Vectant support session. It reduces manual context sharing while keeping the local app as the final authority. Success means users can prove what is available locally, what was requested, what was blocked or redacted, what was actually sent, and how to stop access immediately.

## Brand Personality

Calm, explicit, trustworthy. The product speaks plainly about risk and state, avoids celebratory security copy, and treats transparency as a working control rather than reassurance or decoration.

## Anti-references

- Hidden background agents, remote administration tools, and broad machine-inspection utilities.
- Security theatre that says “connected” without showing the session, workspace, actor, capability, and sent history behind that claim.
- Demo dashboards, static evidence tables, or fake activity that imply an end-to-end capability exists when it does not.
- Generic AI-tool decoration, promotional gradients, glass surfaces, theatrical motion, and vague claims of seamless automation.
- Dense protocol jargon where a direct user-facing explanation can state what happened and whether any bytes left the machine.

## Design Principles

1. Show proof, not reassurance. Every consequential state must expose the real source, actor, scope, decision, and outcome.
2. Make boundaries visible. “Available locally” must never look equivalent to “sent to Vectant,” and browser preview must never imply AI or support read access.
3. Keep the stop controls close. Pause, disconnect, deny, and revoke must remain obvious wherever access is active or requested.
4. Fail closed and explain why. Missing, stale, uncertain, or invalid state becomes a safe denial with zero bytes sent and a useful local event.
5. Preserve earned familiarity. Use native product patterns, restrained motion, consistent component states, and the established Vectant frontend vocabulary.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

All critical states and actions must be understandable without relying on color alone, keyboard reachable, visibly focused, and readable at desktop and reduced-width layouts. Motion is limited to short state feedback and respects reduced-motion preferences. UX acceptance requires users to identify the connected workspace, sent and blocked data, approved ports, preview access boundaries, pause/disconnect controls, redactions, and history deletion without reading documentation.
